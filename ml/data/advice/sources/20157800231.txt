FACTSHEETS FOR FARMERS www.plantwise.org Created in Bangladesh , September 2013 (Revised July 2020) Late Blight of Potato Recognize the problem The late blight, a common disease of potato and tomato, can be devastating unless controlled. It affects leaves, stems and tubers, turning them brown or black and rotten. This disease will significantly reduce yield and cause potatoes to rot in store. Early blight is a similar disease, but early blight causes rings inside the brown spots whereas late blight does not. Early symptoms include greasy looking, irregularly shaped gray spots on the leaves. A ring of white mold can develop around the spots, especially in wet weather. The spots eventually turn dry and papery. Blackened areas may appear on the stems. Tomato fruits can also be infected and will develop large, irregularly shaped, greasy gray spots. Background Potato late blight is caused by fungi ( Phytophthora infestans ). Potatoes are more likely to be affected by blight when the weather is cold and wet, and when the plants are too close together. In cold and wet weather, the disease can kill the whole field of plants within 2-3 days. The disease infects leaves, stems and finally is transmitted to the tubers. The disease can be carried in seed potatoes. Management • Use of resistant or tolerant varieties of potato viz. BARI Alu-46, BARI Alu-53, BARI-Alu-77, BARI Alu-90 and BARI Alu-91 • Cultivation of early variety • Use disease-free/clean seed for planting • Use spacing of 60 x 30 centimetres • When cool and foggy weather starts, preventive measures must be taken by spraying with fungicides, like mancozeb (e.g. Dithane M-45/Indofil M-45/ Haymancozeb) or iprovalicarb and propineb (e.g. Melody Duo) @ 0.2%. Spray weekly if the weather is wet and every two weeks if the weather is dry and cold until two weeks before harvest. Spray when the leaves are dry. • Inspect the field regularly for signs of blight • Irrigation must be stopped as soon as late blight infection is noticed • With the start of late blight infection, spray with metalaxyl and mancozeb, e.g. Ridomil Gold @ 35 ml per 20 litre knapsack sprayer, 2-3 times at 7 days interval. Alternatively, spray with one of the following: ◦ Fenamidone and mancozeb, e.g. Secure @ 2gm/L water ◦ Iprovalicarb and propineb, e.g. Melody Duo @ 2gm + fenamidone and mancozeb, e.g. Secure @ 1gm /L water ◦ Iprovalicarb and propineb e.g. Melody Duo @ 2gm + mancozeb e.g. Dithane M-45 @ 2gm / L water • Remove and destroy all stems and leaves at least two weeks before harvesting, so the disease does not spread from the leaves to the potatoes when harvesting.
