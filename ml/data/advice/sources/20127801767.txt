FACTSHEETS FOR FARMERS www.plantwise.org Created in Rwanda , May 2012 Potato late blight Recognize the problem Potato late blight, also called "indwara y'imvura" or "mildiou" in Kinyarwanda, attacks leaves, stems and tubers. Leaves and stems turn brown and have a burnt appearance. Tuber skin is discoloured with a reddish brown rot underneath the discoloration. Background Potato late blight is caused by a fungus. The best conditions for fungus spread are wet and cold conditions. The fungus survives in the tubers, volunteer plants and discarded piles of potatoes. The fungus is spread between plants by wind, rain, machinery and animals. Management • Use resistant and/or tolerant varieties • Plant certified seed that is free of late blight • Keep the crop free of weeds to reduce moisture around the plants • Wash field equipment and machinery after use • Avoid movement of people and animals in your fields • Destroy all culled and volunteer potatoes • Before you see the fungus you can spray the fungicide mancozeb to prevent the disease, at the rate of 50 grams of fungicide in 20 litres of water. Spray every 2 weeks until maturity. If you see the disease you can spray the fungicide mefenoxam and mancozeb to control it. Spray at the rate of 40 grams in 20 litres of water. Spray once per season. • Add soil to the rows to increase depth of tubers to provide mechanical barrier to fungus penetration to tubers in the soil • Rotate with maize, wheat or beans after each season. Scientific name(s) > Phytophthora infestans The recommendations in this factsheet are relevant to : Ghana, Rwanda Authors : J. B Muhinyuza, M.
