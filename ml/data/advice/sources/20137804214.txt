FACTSHEETS FOR FARMERS www.plantwise.org Created in Kenya , December 2012 Late blight on Tomato Recognize the problem Late blight is a minor disease during the dry season; however it is most serious during cool moist conditions. Spores from the sick plant can move into a clean plant and infect it. At first, the disease causes irregular greenish or water soaked lesions on the leaves, stems and fruits. Leaf spots turn a bluish-grey colour and sometimes when the weather is wet have a white fuzzy growth around the margins of the spots on the undersides of the leaves. Infected leaves turn brown and wither, but stay attached to the plant. Fruits develop watery brownish green spots which develop on the upper half of fruit. The disease leads to a very quick death of the entire plant. Background The disease is spread by water splashes from the soil. The disease can also be spread by overhead irrigation and in overcrowded farms. The disease survives in old crop residues and other plants related to the tomato. There are varieties of tomatoes that are tolerant to the disease. Management Non-chemical control: • Practice crop rotation by avoiding tomato or related crops (e.g. Irish potatoes, capsicum, brinjals etc) for at least 3 to 4 seasons. • Plant resistant varieties. • Transplants should be free of disease. • Stake the plant to keep them off soil. • Mulch the plant to reduce water splashes. • Irrigating during the day can help reduce the severity of the infection because this disease needs to have free water around to spread. Chemical control: • Fungicide sprays should be applied as soon as the disease symptoms are seen, or before if there are prolonged periods of wet weather as these conditions favour the disease. • These chemicals include copper oxychloride, Dimethomorph + Mancozeb, Famoxadone + Cymoxanil, Metalaxyl-M + Mancozeb and Mancozeb.
