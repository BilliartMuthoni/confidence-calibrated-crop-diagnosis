FACTSHEETS FOR FARMERS www.plantwise.org Created in Trinidad and Tobago , November 2011 Bacterial leaf spot Recognize the problem Bacterial leaf spot attacks sweet pepper, hot pepper and tomato. The disease attacks each crop in different ways. On sweet peppers , small, light brown spots appear under the leaf. The spots are angular, round or irregular and they turn dark brown. The spots can be slightly raised. Several spots can occur especially along the leaf margins where moisture settles. They may join and then the affected area dies. Leaves usually fall off. Fruits develop raised spots and cannot be sold. On hot peppers , the disease mainly affects the leaves, with small, light brown round to irregular spots. The spots may grow up to a quarter inch wide and the sick areas may fall, leaving shot-holes. Spots along the leaf margins may join and the affected area dies. The leaves usually fall off. Tomatoes get small wet spots on the leaves. Spots may join and die. The leaves may become yellow and die. Flowers may fall off. Raised spots appear on green fruits. Background This disease can be carried in the seeds of the crop. Once the disease enters the field it is hard to control. The disease is worse in wet conditions. It is more difficult to control during the rainy season. The disease spreads from diseased plants, from the soil and from the splash of water. The bacteria can be spread by touching sick plants. The disease can survive in crop remains. Copper-based fungicides can help to slow the development of the disease. Management • Use disease-free seedlings. • Avoid overhead sprinklers. Use drip irrigation to avoid splashing soil on the plants. • Do not overcrowd the plants in the field. Leave some space between them. • Mulch can reduce the spread of the disease by reducing soil splash. Plastic mulch works well. • After harvest, do not plant sweet peppers, tomato or hot peppers in the same soil. • Use copper-based fungicides in combination with mancozeb at the recommended rates. Spray them every 7 days, or every 14 days if the disease is bad. • Follow the recommendations on the label for the safe period of application of these fungicides before harvesting. • Biological disease control products can also be used.
