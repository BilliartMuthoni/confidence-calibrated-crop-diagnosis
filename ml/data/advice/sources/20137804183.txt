FACTSHEETS FOR FARMERS www.plantwise.org Created in Kenya , October 2012 Late blight or barafu in tomatoes Recognize the problem Late blight, also known as barafu , is caused by a water mould, a fungus-like disease that attacks leaves, stems and fruits. Late blight reduces yields and can quickly kill plants. Affected leaves develop brown uneven patches which become black and quickly spread. Leaves burn out and die. Stems develop uneven black water-soaked patches. The fruit has similar patches near where they attach to the stem. Background The water mould produces spores which survive in soil and in plants (e.g. tomato seeds, crop carry over and weeds). Rain helps to splash spores on to new plants. The disease is also spread by tools and on clothing. Barafu is common during wet and cold weather and spreads rapidly between tomato plants close to each other. Infection starts with lower older leaves close to or touching the soil. Short varieties are most likely to get late blight because of this. Barafu also attacks potato and black nightshade. Management CULTURAL METHODS • Plant tomatoes 90cm by 30cm or 75cm by 45cm or 60cm by 60cm. • Wash tools with clean water after use to avoid spread of the disease. • Weed regularly and remove crops like potatoes and black nightshade. Remove diseased leaves and plant parts from the field. • Do not grow tomatoes every year in the same place: alternate with onion then beans and cabbage. • Keep two suckers per plant for tall varieties like money maker, Anna FI and star 9082 F1. • If you plant short varieties such as Riogrande and Kilele F1 cover the soil with dry grass or wheat straws. Do not use weeds since they could spread pests and diseases. CHEMICAL CONTROL Avoid spraying on windy days and spray at least 4 hours before it rains. Add sticker and spreader to the chemicals to enable them to stay on the plant. • During wet and cold weather, spray preventive fungicide such as mancozeb, copper-based products, etc. • In case of attack, spray fungicides containing metalaxyl or metalaxyl M in addition to mancozeb to halt the spread of the disease.
