FACTSHEETS FOR FARMERS www.plantwise.org Created in Pakistan , September 2013 Potato Late Blight Recognize the problem Late blight is a serious disease which causes yield losses of up to 70%. At first, small brown leaf spots are seen on the edges of the leaves and become blacker at later stages of infection. Finally, the spots unite together, killing the leaf and then the plant. Tuber infection is seen on the skin and causes blackening and softening inside the tuber. A bad smell comes from infected plants and their tubers. Do not confuse this disease with early blight, which has ‘ target board ’ concentric rings around spots on the leaf. Background Potato late blight is caused by a fungus-like organism that lives in soil and is carried by wind. It can therefore spread very fast and kills all the plants in just a few days. The disease is most commonly seen in December, when temperatures are low and humidity is high. The disease can be spread from one field to another through contact with people or agricultural tools. Management • Sort out or reject the infected tubers at the harvesting/storage stage • Disinfect agricultural tools: use bleach solution at 2g/litre of water • When there are low temperatures and high humidity, apply a preventive spray on the crop, e.g. mancozeb @ 2g /litre of water with 10-14 day intervals • Avoid too much irrigation during low temperature days • Cultivate resistant/tolerant varieties, e.g. FSD White, available at Punjab Seed Corporation • 2-3 sprays of (mancozeb+ metalaxyl @ 2g /litre of water) just after the appearance of disease to overcome further spread Scientific name(s) > Phytophthora infestans The recommendations in this factsheet are relevant to : Pakistan Authors : Yasar Saleem Khan, Amna Palwasha, M.
