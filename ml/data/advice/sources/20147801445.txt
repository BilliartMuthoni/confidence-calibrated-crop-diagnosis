FACTSHEETS FOR FARMERS www.plantwise.org Created in Nepal , September 2012 Cultural Management of Late Blight of Potato Recognize the problem The disease attacks all parts of the plant. It initially appears as small blackish/brown burnt areas on the leaf surface. The symptoms rapidly increase in size and cover a larger area of the leaves and stem. It causes heavy losses, sometimes more than 80%, and causes the potatoes to rot in store. The disease spreads very fast, so it is not easy to control once in the crop. Background Warm and humid or moist and cloudy conditions are favourable for the spread of the disease. Planting diseased tubers will bring the disease to the field. Early planting also favours the spread of the disease. Cultural practices should be used, along with chemical control to reduce the disease spread. Management • Use disease-free certified seed tubers, suggested by the agriculturist • Planting in February is desirable • Follow crop rotation in infested areas. Don't use susceptible crops like, tomato, brinjal and chilli. • Watch the crop regularly and if the disease is found, destroy the affected area by burning • Irrigate the field early in the morning so that the leaves do not remain wet for too long • Digging and ridging is necessary to prevent the disease attacking the stems • Remove all the aerial parts 2-3 weeks before harvesting. These can be used for composting. • After harvest, separate the diseased tubers then store the healthy tubers in a shaded and cool place • Don't wash the tubers before storage as this will spread the disease • Frequently monitor the tubers in storage and remove any infested ones Scientific name(s) > Phytophthora infestans The recommendations in this factsheet are relevant to : Nepal Authors : Nathuni Sahu Regional Plant Protection Laboratory tel: +977 21 528956, +977 9852027359 Edited by Plantwise.
